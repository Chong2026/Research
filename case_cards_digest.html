Representative for Children and Youth v. British Columbia (Children and Family Development), 2010 BCSC 697 (BCSC, 2010-05-14) Q2
Open decision

Summary. This case is a petition by the Representative for Children and Youth seeking to enforce her right to information under s. 10 of the Representative for Children and Youth Act against the Office of the Premier and the Ministry of Children and Family Development. Madam Justice S. Griffin of the BC Supreme Court held that the right was justiciable and granted declaratory relief and an order in the nature of mandamus compelling production of Cabinet submissions.

Outcome. Petition granted; declaration and mandamus ordered.

Q2 · contrarycourt's holding

The court held that the petitioner had a justiciable remedy and awarded mandamus, distinguishing the Supreme Court of Canada's Auditor General decision where mandamus was denied because the dispute was non-justiciable and reserved to Parliament. The passages provided therefore show a case rejecting justiciability concerns as a bar to mandamus.

para 54
In this case, I conclude that the Legislature clearly has expressed its intention. Its intention is not to make itself the exclusive forum for the resolution of disputes in the RCYA; its intention is quite the opposite.
para 116
In conclusion, I find that the requirements of an order for mandamus have been established by the petitioner in this case. Such an order will go against the personal respondents.
British Columbia Nurses' Union v. Attorney General of British Columbia, 2008 BCSC 321 (BCSC, 2008-03-18) Q3 Q4
Open decision

Summary. The British Columbia Nurses' Union sought mandamus and declarations to compel the Medical Services Commission to enforce the Medicare Protection Act. Kelleher J. of the British Columbia Supreme Court dismissed the Rule 19(24) application but denied the Union public interest standing.

Outcome. The petition was dismissed because the Union was denied public interest standing, although the application to strike it under Rule 19(24) was dismissed.

Q3 · contrarycourt's holding

The passages provided do not address caseload or staff resources or delay, but the court rejected the broader premise that a commission's control over enforcement makes its conduct non-justiciable: it held that compliance with the Act is not a matter of administrative discretion and that while the Commission may choose how to ensure compliance, it has no discretion whether to enforce the Act.

para 30
Compliance with the Act is not a matter of administrative discretion.
para 30
It does not have a discretion to decide whether to enforce the Act.
Q4 · qualifiescourt's holding

The court linked justiciability to public interest standing and treated a clear statutory duty as justiciable, while leaving open mandamus to compel performance of that duty; it also indicated that mandamus cannot direct how a body exercises its discretion.

para 27
The court held that questions of law are clearly justificiable and will raise a serious issue even if they are embedded in political context.
para 29
The petitioner points to a precise piece of legislation that imposes a duty on the Commission and raises a justiciable issue.
para 30
A Court can compel a body, such as the Board, to carry out its duty. It cannot direct a body, whether it has discretion in respect of mode, as to how it shall carry out the duty.
para 60
courts have left open the availability of mandamus to compel a public officer to exercise a duty.
para 69
It cannot be determined at this stage that the court lacks jurisdiction to issue orders of mandamus or the declarations sought.
